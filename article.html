What to check in your data before an AI pilot

An AI pilot is only as useful as the data behind it. A short review of your sources saves weeks of chasing results nobody trusts.

Most AI pilots stall for a simple reason: the data was never checked against the question it was meant to answer. A short review before you start keeps the pilot focused and its results easier to trust.


Here is the checklist we use with teams before connecting the first source. To see how we work with clients, visit our about page.



Know where each source comes from


For every source you plan to connect, the team should be able to answer three questions:


  • Who owns it and keeps it up to date.
  • How often it changes and when it last did.
  • Which decision it is meant to inform.


Ask the people who use it every day


The analysts and operators who work with a dataset know its gaps better than any schema. Ask them what they double-check before trusting a number, and write those checks down.




A clear question is worth more than another data source.





Reviewing sources with the people who use them catches problems early.




Start with a narrow pilot


Once the sources are clear, keep the first pilot small enough to review by hand:


  • Pick one workflow and one decision to support.
  • Compare AI outputs with what the team would have concluded.
  • Record every correction so the next round improves.

A small pilot that people trust is a better starting point than a large one nobody uses.
